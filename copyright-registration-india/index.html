Copyright Registration in India — Form XIV, Second Schedule Fees and the 30-Day Objection Window

Quick answer: Copyright exists from creation, but registration on the Copyright Office register is the evidence that wins disputes, marketplace takedowns and investor diligence. You file Form XIV with a Statement of Particulars on copyright.gov.in, one application per work: ₹500 for a literary, dramatic, musical or artistic work (software counts as literary), ₹2,000 for an artistic work used on goods or services (a logo — with the Trade Marks Registry's TM-C no-objection) and for a sound recording, ₹5,000 for a cinematograph film. A diary number issues on filing, a mandatory 30-day objection window follows, then examination; discrepancy letters must be answered within 30 days. We file, clear discrepancies and pair the logo with the trademark.

Process and timeline

  1. Form XIV + Statement of Particulars (and Further Particulars) on copyright.gov.in; upload copies of the work (source code excerpt for software — first and last 10 pages or full, as prescribed); NOC of the author/publisher where the applicant differs
  2. Diary number issues on filing
  3. 30-day mandatory objection window for third parties
  4. Examination; a discrepancy letter must be answered within 30 days (extendable by 30); a hearing follows if an objection is lodged
  5. Registration certificate — typically 2–6 months in all, longer with objections

Term and what registration does for you

  • Term: author's life + 60 years for literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works; 60 years from publication for films, sound recordings, photographs and government/anonymous works
  • Registered certificates are prima facie evidence in infringement suits (s.48), accepted by Amazon/Flipkart brand registries and app stores for takedowns, and demanded in investor and acquisition diligence
  • A logo gets both protections: copyright as an artistic work and a trademark in the right class — file them together

Frequently asked questions

What is the copyright registration fee in India?

₹500 per literary, dramatic, musical or artistic work (software included); ₹2,000 for an artistic work used on goods or services and for a sound recording; ₹5,000 for a cinematograph film — Second Schedule, Copyright Rules 2013.

How much does software copyright registration cost?

₹500 per work — software is a literary work under the Act; pages quoting ₹5,000 have confused it with films.

Why does a logo cost ₹2,000?

An artistic work used or capable of use in relation to goods or services pays ₹2,000 and needs a TM-C no-objection from the Trade Marks Registry before the Copyright Office registers it.

How long does copyright registration take?

A diary number issues on filing, a mandatory 30-day objection window follows, then examination — typically 2–6 months in all, longer if a discrepancy or objection arises.

Is copyright registration mandatory?

No — copyright arises on creation; registration is evidence (s.48) that matters in court, marketplace takedowns and diligence.

How long does copyright last?

Author's life plus 60 years for literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works; 60 years from publication for films, sound recordings and photographs.

Can I register a book and its cover in one application?

No — the text (literary) and the cover (artistic) are separate works and separate ₹500 applications.

What if the Copyright Office sends a discrepancy letter?

Reply within 30 days (extendable by 30) with the corrected particulars or documents; unanswered letters lead to the application being treated as abandoned.
