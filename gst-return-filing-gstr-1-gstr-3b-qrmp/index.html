GST Return Filing — GSTR-1, GSTR-3B, QRMP, Composition and Annual Return (GSTR-9/9C)

Quick answer: Regular GST taxpayers file GSTR-1 (outward supplies) by the 11th and GSTR-3B (summary and tax payment) by the 20th of the following month. Taxpayers with aggregate turnover up to ₹5 crore can opt into QRMP: GSTR-1 quarterly by the 13th, GSTR-3B quarterly by the 22nd or 24th depending on the state group, with tax for the first two months paid through PMT-06 by the 25th and optional B2B uploads through the IFF by the 13th. Composition taxpayers file CMP-08 quarterly by the 18th. The annual return GSTR-9 is due 31 December after the year (mandatory above ₹2 crore, optional below) and the GSTR-9C reconciliation applies above ₹5 crore. Delhi, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and Bihar are in the 24th-date group.

QRMP, composition and what changes the filing route

  • QRMP is available up to ₹5 crore aggregate turnover (PAN level); you choose it quarter by quarter on the portal, and it cuts returns from 24 to 8 a year plus the monthly PMT-06 challan
  • Composition is for small suppliers under the turnover ceiling notified for the category — ₹1.5 crore for goods traders and manufacturers (lower in some special-category states) and ₹50 lakh for eligible service providers; no input tax credit and no inter-state outward supply, with CMP-08 each quarter and GSTR-4 yearly
  • Interest on late tax payment runs at 18% a year under Section 50; late fees for GSTR-1, GSTR-3B and GSTR-9 are fixed by notification by turnover band and are computed at filing
  • Mismatch control: GSTR-1 flows to the buyer's GSTR-2B and GSTR-3B is compared to it — a gap between GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B is the commonest trigger for an ASMT-10 scrutiny notice or a GST notice

Annual return and reconciliation

GSTR-9 reconciles the year's GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B figures with the books; GSTR-9C, for turnover above ₹5 crore, reconciles the annual return with the audited financial statements and is self-certified. Differences in ITC, turnover and HSN summaries are the points officers ask about later in audit or assessment.

Frequently asked questions

When is GSTR-1 due?

The 11th of the next month for monthly filers and the 13th of the month after the quarter for QRMP filers; the IFF for the first two months of a quarter is optional by the 13th.

When is GSTR-3B due?

The 20th of the next month for monthly filers; QRMP filers file quarterly by the 22nd or the 24th depending on the state group — Delhi, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh and Bihar use the 24th.

Who can opt for QRMP?

Registered taxpayers with aggregate annual turnover up to ₹5 crore, who then file GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B quarterly and pay tax for the first two months through PMT-06 by the 25th.

What is PMT-06?

The challan used by QRMP filers to deposit tax for the first two months of a quarter, by the 25th of the following month.

Is GSTR-9 mandatory?

It is mandatory above ₹2 crore aggregate turnover and optional below it; it is due by 31 December after the financial year.

Who files GSTR-9C?

Taxpayers with turnover above ₹5 crore, along with GSTR-9 by 31 December, as a self-certified reconciliation with the audited accounts.

What are the composition scheme returns?

CMP-08 each quarter by the 18th for payment of tax and the annual GSTR-4; the turnover ceiling depends on the category and state.

What interest applies on late GST payment?

Interest at 18% a year on the tax paid late under Section 50, plus a late fee on the return fixed by notification.
