ISO Certification Consultant

Quick answer: ISO certification is issued by private, accredited certification bodies — not any government department. We prepare your documentation and systems, then coordinate the audit with a genuinely accredited body, so your certificate actually holds up with tender committees and buyers.

Last verified 4 October 2026 — rules on this page checked against the current notifications. We update it the day a rule changes.

Said plainly: ISO certification is not a government scheme and no government department issues it. It's a private, standards-based certification — and the honest value of a consultant here is preparing your systems properly and choosing a genuinely accredited certification body, not promising a shortcut.

What we actually do

  • Gap analysis — where your current practices fall short of the chosen standard
  • Documentation — building the quality/environmental/food-safety manual, procedures and records the standard requires
  • Staff training — so the system is actually followed, not just written down
  • Internal audit & management review — before an external auditor ever sees it
  • Certification-body selection — an accredited body (NABCB or another IAF-member accreditation), not an unaccredited "instant ISO" provider whose certificate tender committees increasingly reject
  • Audit coordination — Stage 1 and Stage 2 audits, closing non-conformities, and annual surveillance support to keep the certificate valid

"IAF-accredited" changed meaning on 1 January 2026

The International Accreditation Forum (IAF) and ILAC merged into Global Accreditation Cooperation Incorporated, operational from 1 January 2026, which took over their multilateral arrangements. Existing IAF MLA marks remain valid through the transition. The correct 2026 description of a worthwhile certificate is: issued by a certification body accredited by a Global Accreditation Cooperation MRA signatory such as NABCB. A sales page still saying only "IAF-approved" without that chain is a warning sign.

How to check an ISO certificate is genuine — 5 steps

  1. Two logos on the certificate: the certification body's and its accreditation body's (NABCB guidance: the AB logo should appear alongside the CB logo). One logo, or a "registrar" nobody can name, fails here.
  2. Accreditation body is a signatory — NABCB (India) or another member listed by the Global Accreditation Cooperation / former IAF MLA.
  3. Certification body is actually accredited by that AB for that standard — check the AB's public register (NABCB publishes its accredited CBs by scheme).
  4. The certificate number validates on IAF CertSearch (iafcertsearch.org) or the CB's own verification page, with matching scope, site and dates.
  5. Surveillance is live — a three-year certificate with no annual surveillance audit on record is lapsed even if the paper says 2027.

Doubts about an Indian certificate can be referred to NABCB (nabcb@qcin.org). ISO itself does not certify anyone — a certificate "from ISO" does not exist.

Frequently asked questions

How do I verify an ISO certificate online?

Check the certificate number on IAF CertSearch (iafcertsearch.org) or the certification body's verification page; confirm the body is accredited for that standard by a Global Accreditation Cooperation (formerly IAF) signatory such as NABCB; and confirm both logos appear on the certificate. If any link in that chain is missing, the certificate will not survive a tender scrutiny.

Is my IAF-accredited certificate still valid after the 2026 merger?

Yes — IAF and ILAC became Global Accreditation Cooperation Incorporated on 1 January 2026 and existing IAF MLA marks stay valid through the transition. What changes is the name to look for on accreditation registers.

Does ISO itself issue certificates?

No. ISO publishes the standards; independent certification bodies audit and certify, and accreditation bodies (NABCB in India) accredit those certifiers. 'ISO-issued' certificates are a red flag.

Is ISO certification a government registration?

No. ISO standards are published by the International Organization for Standardization, and certificates are issued by private certification bodies — ideally ones accredited by NABCB (India) or another IAF-member accreditation body. No ministry or government department issues an ISO certificate.

How do I check if an ISO certificate is genuine?

Confirm that the certification body itself is accredited — by NABCB or another IAF-member accreditation body — and verify the certificate against that accreditation body's public database. A growing number of 'instant ISO' certificates from non-accredited bodies are now being rejected by tender committees.

Which ISO standard do I actually need?

ISO 9001 (quality management) is the one most tenders and buyers ask for. ISO 14001 covers environmental management, ISO 22000 covers food safety, and ISO 45001 covers occupational health and safety — the right one depends on your industry and what your buyers or tenders specifically require.

Is ISO a substitute for BIS, FSSAI or Pollution NOC?

No — these are separate statutory requirements under Indian law and remain mandatory regardless of ISO status. ISO is a voluntary management-system certification that complements, but never replaces, your legal compliance obligations.

What does the certification process actually involve?

A gap analysis against the chosen standard, building or updating your documented procedures and records, staff training, an internal audit and management review, then a Stage 1 and Stage 2 audit by your chosen accredited certification body, closing any non-conformities they raise, and ongoing annual surveillance audits to keep the certificate active.
