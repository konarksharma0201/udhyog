Liquor & Excise Licensing Consultant — Delhi

Quick answer: Delhi's current excise regime (in force since September 2022) has been extended to 31 March 2027 — the drafted "new" policy is not yet notified. Private retail vend isn't available to individuals; the real opportunity is L-17 restaurant/bar, L-15/L-16 hotel, L-28 club, L-1 wholesale, and P-10 event-permit licensing, all filed via the e-Abkari portal.

Status as of August 2026 — what's actually in force: Delhi's duty-based excise policy (Sept 2022) has been extended a fourth time, now through 31 March 2027. Retail liquor sale remains a government-corporation monopoly (DTTDC, DSIIDC, DSCSC, DCCWS). A new "premium modern stores" policy is drafted but not notified — don't plan around it yet.

The application process

  1. Eligibility & category check. Confirm which license actually fits your premises and business model — this is where we see the most wasted time, since retail-vend enquiries currently have no path forward under the present policy.
  2. Documentation. Layout plans, affidavits, Tourism approval (for L-17/L-15/L-16), fire NOC, and the category-specific document set — incomplete files are the most common rejection ground.
  3. e-Abkari filing. All applications go through eabkari.delhi.gov.in — create an EXD ID, select the form, upload documents, pay fees.
  4. Inspection & objection window. Premises inspection against Rule 51 distance norms, plus a public-objection window for hotel licenses.
  5. Grant & annual renewal. Licenses run on the excise year and need renewal — we track your date so it never lapses into an enforcement problem.
Enforcement is active right now: per a May 2025 report, Delhi Excise ran a special drive against unlicensed premises — around 1,000 hotels/clubs/restaurants were flagged as serving without proper licenses, with dozens raided and multiple FIRs registered in a single quarter. If you're operating without the right license, that's a live risk, not a hypothetical one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I open a private liquor shop (retail vend) in Delhi?

No — under the current excise regime (extended to 31 March 2027), retail liquor sale in Delhi is restricted to four government corporations (DTTDC, DSIIDC, DSCSC, DCCWS). Private individuals cannot get a retail vend licence right now. Private opportunity is in restaurant/bar (L-17), hotel (L-15/L-16), club (L-28), wholesale (L-1), and event permits (P-10/P-13).

Which license does my restaurant need to serve alcohol in Delhi?

An independent restaurant needs an L-17 licence (with the L-17F add-on for foreign liquor). Your premises must be in a commercial area, Tourism-approved, and meet Rule 51 distance norms (100m from schools, religious places and hospitals), with fire and municipal approvals in place. Hotels use L-15/L-16 instead; clubs use L-28.

How much does a restaurant liquor license cost in Delhi?

L-17 fees are annual and scale with your seating and category; HCR-category fees (L-17/L-18/L-19/L-20) were raised 10% for 2026-27. We give you a current, category-specific estimate after reviewing your premises rather than quoting a generic number that may already be outdated.

How do I get a liquor permit for a wedding or party in Delhi?

Apply for a P-10 permit — Rs 5,000 for most venues, Rs 15,000 for banquet halls, motels and farmhouses. Liquor must be purchased from an authorised Delhi vend (keep the original bill), served only to guests above 25, and kept screened from public view. We handle the e-Abkari filing so it's approved before your date.

Where are Delhi liquor license applications filed?

Online, through the e-Abkari portal (eabkari.delhi.gov.in) — physical-only submissions were discontinued in 2017. The flow is: create an EXD ID, select the license form, upload documents and layout plans, pay fees, then clear a premises inspection before the license is granted.

My liquor license application was rejected — what can I do?

Rejections commonly cite eligibility issues, pending investigations, distance-rule violations, or incomplete documents. Delhi's courts have repeatedly directed applicants to first appeal to the Appellate/Financial Commissioner under the Excise Act rather than going straight to court. We diagnose the actual ground for rejection and file a reasoned appeal.

Is Delhi's new liquor policy in force yet?

Not as of August 2026. A new policy (modern/premium stores) is still in draft under a government committee. The current duty-based regime — in place since September 2022 — has been extended repeatedly and now runs to 31 March 2027. We track this and update clients the moment anything actually changes, rather than reacting to rumours.

What is the legal drinking age and personal possession limit in Delhi?

The legal drinking age in Delhi is 25 (Section 23, Delhi Excise Act, 2009) — is strictly 25 (Section 23, Delhi Excise Act, 2009).. Without a permit, you may possess up to 9 litres of Indian/foreign spirits or 18 litres of wine/beer; to hold more, you need an L-30 personal permit.

Do I need a permit to serve alcohol at a house party in Delhi?

At your own residence — no permit is needed to serve guests and family, as long as the liquor stays within your personal possession limit. The moment the event moves outside your residence — a farmhouse, banquet hall or any other premises — a P-10 permit is required even if the quantity is within the possession limit. That distinction catches many hosts off guard.

What is the P-13 permit, and how is it different from P-10?

P-13 is for premises that already hold a liquor licence — hotels, restaurants and clubs hosting a one-day function, at a Rs 5,000 permit fee. P-10 is for everyone else: serving liquor at a party, wedding or event on unlicensed premises, at Rs 5,000 for general venues and Rs 15,000 for motels, banquet halls and farmhouses. Note the service age at such events is 25 years, not 21.

What is the L-30 licence, and who actually needs it?

L-30 is Delhi's home-stock licence: if you want to keep liquor at your residence above the individual free possession limit (9 litres of spirits / 18 litres of wine and beer), you apply for an L-30 grant and renew it periodically with the Excise Department. It's relevant for collectors and serious home bars — without it, excess stock at home is an excise violation.

Who is eligible for the L-28 club licence?

Only a club formally registered with the Registrar of Firms or Registrar of Cooperative Societies — and the licence permits service of liquor strictly to the club's own members within club premises, with stock procured from authorised sources. Membership records and registration documents are the core of the application file; we prepare both.
