NRI Selling Property in India (2026) — Capital Gains, Buyer's TDS & Repatriation

Quick answer: From 1 April 2026 an NRI's gain on Indian property held over 24 months is taxed at 12.5% without indexation — the 20%-with-indexation option for pre-23 July 2024 purchases is available only to resident individuals and HUFs, not NRIs. The buyer must hold a TAN and deduct tax on the full sale consideration (13% / 14.30% / 14.95% with surcharge and cess) unless the seller obtains a lower-deduction certificate (Form 128, formerly Form 13). Up to USD 1 million a year can be repatriated from the NRO account with Form 15CA and a CA's 15CB. We get the certificate before the deed, not the refund after it.

Last verified 4 October 2026 — rules on this page checked against the current notifications. We update it the day a rule changes.

The rate — and the indexation point most pages get wrong

  • Long-term (held > 24 months): 12.5% without indexation. The option to pay 20% with indexation on land or buildings bought before 23 July 2024 belongs to resident individuals and HUFs only. An NRI cannot elect it — pages offering NRIs "12.5% or 20% with indexation, whichever is lower" are wrong.
  • Short-term (≤ 24 months): slab rates.
  • Surcharge on LTCG is capped at 15%; health and education cess 4%.

Why the certificate matters: there is no ₹50 lakh threshold for an NRI seller and the buyer withholds on the whole sale price, not the gain. On a ₹2 crore Gurugram flat bought for ₹1.4 crore, default TDS is ₹26–29.9 lakh against an actual tax of roughly ₹7.5 lakh on the ₹60 lakh gain — the difference sits with the department until a refund 12–18 months later. A Form 128 lower-deduction certificate from the Assessing Officer, applied for before the sale deed, fixes TDS on the computed gain instead. Allow 30+ days.

The sequence, buyer and seller together

  1. Seller: compute gain, apply for Form 128 (TAN of buyer, agreement to sell, cost proof, PAN); 30+ days
  2. Buyer: obtain TAN (mandatory when paying a non-resident); deduct at the certificate rate or the full rate
  3. Deposit by the 7th of the following month; file the non-resident TDS statement for the quarter; issue the certificate to the seller
  4. Seller: reinvest within the windows (new residential house; specified bonds within 6 months) if claiming the exemptions, and file the return by 31 July
  5. Repatriation: up to USD 1 million per financial year from the NRO account, with Form 15CA and a CA's Form 15CB; DTAA relief needs a Tax Residency Certificate and Form 10F — most treaties still let India tax Indian property gains, so relief is usually a foreign tax credit at home

Frequently asked questions

Can an NRI choose 20% with indexation on property bought before 23 July 2024?

No. That option (now s.197(3) of the 2025 Act) is for resident individuals and HUFs only. An NRI pays 12.5% on the long-term gain without indexation.

Is TDS deducted on the sale price or the capital gain?

On the full sale consideration unless the seller holds a lower-deduction certificate (Form 128) — then on the amount the certificate specifies, usually the computed gain.

Does the buyer need a TAN to buy from an NRI?

Yes. Payments to a non-resident fall under Section 393(2) (formerly 195); the buyer deducts, deposits and files the non-resident TDS statement under a TAN — PAN-based Form 141 (formerly 26QB) is for resident sellers only.

What is the new form for a lower TDS certificate?

Form 128 under the Income-tax Rules 2026, replacing Form 13; apply to the jurisdictional Assessing Officer (international taxation) before the sale deed.

Which exemptions can an NRI claim?

The successors to Sections 54 (reinvest in a residential house in India), 54EC (specified bonds within six months) and 54F (other long-term assets into a house) — Sections 82, 85 and 86 of the 2025 Act, subject to their conditions.

How much can be repatriated after selling property?

Up to USD 1 million per financial year from the NRO account, including property sale proceeds, with Form 15CA and a CA's Form 15CB and the bank's documentation.

Does a DTAA exempt the gain in India?

Rarely — most Indian treaties give India the right to tax gains on Indian immovable property; the treaty's value is usually a credit for Indian tax in the country of residence, claimed with a TRC and Form 10F.

Can excess TDS be refunded?

Yes, through the seller's return — but it takes a filing season and processing time. A Form 128 certificate avoids the lock-up altogether.
