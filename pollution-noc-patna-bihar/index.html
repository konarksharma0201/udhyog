Pollution NOC Patna & Bihar — CTE & CTO

Quick answer: Units in Patna & Bihar take Consent to Establish and Consent to Operate from the Bihar State Pollution Control Board (BSPCB) (not DPCC). Category under the 2025 CPCB schedule decides whether you need a full consent or only an intimation, what controls you install and how long consent lasts. We classify first, prepare the technical file, and handle the inspection.

What is specific here: For a new Bihar unit, BSPCB consent is one of three files that have to move together — BIADA allotment, PMFME or state incentive, and consent — and the bank will not disburse against a plot that cannot be operated. Food processors are typically green or orange depending on boiler and effluent; we classify before the DPR is finalised so the project cost includes the ETP if one is needed.

We serve units across the BIADA estates at Patliputra, Fatuha and Bihta, Hajipur across the river, Didarganj logistics clusters, and the rice, makhana, flour and food-processing units that make up most of Bihar's new consent applications.

Documents required for Consent to Establish / Operate

  • Application on the board's online portal with the prescribed fee (investment-linked)
  • Proof of the entity and the site — incorporation/partnership documents, lease or allotment letter
  • Site plan and layout showing process areas, storage, ETP/STP and stack locations
  • Process flow diagram with raw materials, products, by-products and quantities
  • Water balance — source, consumption, effluent generation and treatment; air emissions and control equipment
  • Capital investment certificate (CA-certified) for fee computation
  • Hazardous-waste details and authorisation application where applicable; bio-medical waste for clinics
  • For CTO: completion report, monitoring/analysis reports, and compliance with CTE conditions
  • Land-use/CLU proof and, for industrial estates, the allotment conditions

Exact requirements vary by category and state; we confirm the list for your case before filing so the application is not returned.

New in Bihar (23 September 2026): the revamped Bihar Single Window now integrates 62 services across 9 departments — BSPCB consent, Fire NOC, Legal Metrology, Excise & Registration, Water Resources, Energy (DISCOM connections), Environment & Forest, Road Construction and Revenue & Land Reforms — so a new unit's consent, fire, land and power files can move on one dashboard with the BIADA and incentive applications.

Frequently asked questions

Which board issues pollution NOC in Patna & Bihar?

The Bihar State Pollution Control Board (BSPCB), through BSPCB's online consent management system, with Bihar Single Window Clearance (swc2.bihar.gov.in) for new industrial units — Head office: Parivesh Bhawan, Patliputra Industrial Area, Patna (800010); regional offices across the state. Delhi units go to DPCC instead; the two systems, categories and fees are not interchangeable even for neighbouring premises.

What is the difference between CTE and CTO?

Consent to Establish is required before you build or install — it approves the plan, location and pollution-control design. Consent to Operate is required before you start production — it confirms the controls are built and working. CTO is renewed periodically; CTE is a one-time approval per establishment or expansion.

What are red, orange, green and white categories?

CPCB classifies industries by a Pollution Index. Red (60+) carries the heaviest conditions and shortest consent terms; orange (41–59) and green (21–40) progressively lighter; white (up to 20) is the lowest-impact set, now handled under a simplified intimation regime in states that have adopted the 2025 schedule rather than full consent. Your category decides fees, inspection, validity and whether you need an ETP.

What documents are needed?

Typically: proof of the entity and the site (lease/allotment), site plan and layout, process flow with raw materials and products, water balance and effluent/emission details, the pollution-control plan (ETP/STP/stack), capital investment figures (fees are investment-linked in most states), and for CTO the completion and monitoring reports. We prepare the technical annexures so the file is not returned.

How long does pollution consent take?

Portal service standards run from a few weeks for green to 60–90 days for orange and red after inspection; actual time depends on inspection scheduling and whether the file is complete. We track the file through the regional office rather than waiting on the portal status.

Do I need consent for an office, warehouse or restaurant?

Pure offices generally do not; warehouses, restaurants with DG sets, hotels and most workshops usually do, depending on state schedules and DG capacity. Many businesses either file unnecessarily or skip a required consent — we classify first.
