American Embassy School, New Delhi — Admission & Fees Guide

Quick answer: The American Embassy School (Chandragupta Marg, Chanakyapuri) is a non-profit AP/IB school founded in 1952, with 70+ nationalities on a Joseph Allen Stein-designed campus. Its official 2026-27 tuition runs from USD 22,820 (PreK) to USD 39,490 (Grades 9-12), plus a one-time registration capital levy of USD 16,500.

Independent information page. Udyog Growth is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or an agent of this school. We do not guarantee admission and, in line with the Delhi Directorate of Education's March 2025 circular, we do not charge any donation, capitation or “facilitation” fee tied to an admission outcome. Fee figures below are dated and sourced as shown — always verify current fees and criteria directly with the school and with the DoE at edudel.nic.in.

Why admission here is exclusive

AES admits through four defined priority categories, with seats offered by application date within each category — primarily serving the diplomatic and expatriate community, with some Indian students. Very high official fees (below) reflect its non-profit international-school model.

Frequently asked questions

How much is American Embassy School tuition?

Per AES's official 2026-27 schedule: USD 22,820 for PreK, USD 32,260 for KG-Grade 5, USD 37,470 for Grades 6-8, and USD 39,490 for Grades 9-12, plus a one-time registration capital levy of USD 16,500 (PK4-12) and an application fee of USD 800.

Can Indian students attend AES?

Yes, though the school primarily serves the diplomatic and expatriate community; some Indian students are admitted within its priority-category framework.

What are AES's admission priority categories?

Applicants are processed within four defined priority categories, with seats offered by application date within each category, rather than a single open pool.

Which curriculum does AES follow?

Advanced Placement (AP) and International Baccalaureate (IB).

Where is the American Embassy School located?

Chandragupta Marg, Chanakyapuri, New Delhi, on a campus designed by architect Joseph Allen Stein.
